Abstract—This work presents the design and 
development of a compact, wideband, and conformal 
antenna and its array tailored for EW application in 
next-generation missile and unmanned aerial vehicle 
(UAV) platforms. A tapered slot (Vivaldi-based) antenna 
architecture is employed to achieve wideband 
performance across the frequency range of 3–18 GHz, 
supporting high-resolution angle-of-arrival (AOA) 
detection and precise direction finding. To accommodate 
modern aerodynamic constraints, the array is designed 
on thin, flexible substrates such as Rogers laminates, 
allowing seamless integration onto curved surfaces, 
including hemispherical domes and UAV bodies. The 
conformal array configuration ensures mechanical 
adaptability across diverse platforms while preserving 
electromagnetic performance.  
I. INTRODUCTION 
The Vivaldi antenna, originally proposed by Gibson [1], is a 
type of end-fire traveling wave antenna known for its use of 
printed circuit technology, which simplifies both its 
fabrication and structural reliability. Its broadband behavior 
and end-fire radiation characteristics make it suitable for a 
range of applications, including satellite communication, 
weather monitoring, and surveillance systems. Despite these 
advantages, a single Vivaldi antenna element typically 
offers limited gain, prompting the use of antenna arrays to 
achieve higher performance levels. Early implementations 
predominantly used planar array configurations. The 
operating frequency of the Vivaldi antenna is primarily 
influenced by the geometrical parameters of the radiating 
element—specifically, its length, width, and tapered 
profile—which directly affect its radiation efficiency [2], 
[3]. As a result, Vivaldi arrays have become popular for 
their broadband capabilities and ease of integration [4].  
Several design enhancements, such as the inclusion of 
arc-shaped slots, uniform slot-edge structures, and dielectric 
lenses, have been explored to improve gain and reduce size. 
However, these modifications often introduce greater 
complexity and lead to larger physical dimensions [5], [6]. 
In recent years, conformal antenna arrays have emerged as a 
promising alternative due to their ability to reduce 
aerodynamic drag, minimize radar cross-section, improve 
angular coverage, and lower structural costs [3]. 
 
In this paper, the designs of a planar and conformal Vivaldi-
like antenna and its array of 4 such elements are designed 
and discussed. It operates in a wide frequency range with  
 
 
minimal form factor and achieves a consistent impedance 
match and gain over a 3–18 GHz range. 
II. ANTENNA SELECTION 
Ultra-wideband antennas exhibit a wide impedance 
bandwidth but not necessarily a stable radiation pattern, as 
required in most radar applications. Moreover, in addition to 
the large bandwidth and speciﬁc radiation pattern 
characteristics, the installation space environment usually 
impacts the antenna design and performance, as  
 
it mainly constrains the operating frequency range. As a 
result, miniaturization techniques need to be implemented to 
save the installation space and to ensure an optimal trade-off 
between the antenna size and the radiation characteristics. 
For UWB radiating elements, this challenge is even harder 
to fulﬁll, as gain, beam width, and efﬁciency should be 
properly guaranteed. To this aim, several research activities, 
in both academic and industrial ﬁelds, are in progress for the 
identiﬁcation of new wideband antenna geometries 
satisfying the above system requirements and constraints in 
an increasingly extended frequency band. In particular, they 
pertain to the planar antennas and they are focused on the 
improvement of both frequency band and gain uniformity, 
as well as on the polarization management capabilities, size, 
and weight reduction.  
 
Wideband antennas like Vivaldi, spiral, sinuous, and LPDA 
are crucial for various applications due to their ability to 
operate effectively across a wide range of frequencies. 
Broad bandwidth is essential for high data rate 
communication, radar systems, and other applications 
requiring the transmission and reception of signals across a 
wide spectrum. UWB antennas can be broadly divided into 
two categories: 
(i) The Type-1 wideband antennas are produced by 
stimulated resonant modes that are either continuous or 
multiple. Spiral and log periodic antennas are two examples 
of these antennas [7].  
(ii) The Type-2 wideband antennas are produced by 
allowing emission from a limited wavelength area. Common

The increasing demand for multi-band and ultra-wideband 
systems has accelerated the need for antennas capable of 
maintaining stable performance over broad frequency 
ranges. Beyond the popular Vivaldi antenna, alternative 
designs such as spiral, sinuous, and log-periodic dipole 
arrays (LPDAs) offer diverse capabilities suited to various 
applications. While selecting the antenna type, all its 
advantages and disadvantages need to be analyzed.  
(a) Spiral Antenna: 
The spiral antennas are a type of wide-band antenna that 
generate circularly polarized waves and can be easily 
integrated with systems. They are a very suitable choice 
because of their frequency-independent nature.  This is the 
class whose radiation pattern and impedance are practically 
independent of frequency for all frequencies above the 
minimum cut-off value [9]. The advantages include 
relatively easy impedance matching, better radiation 
efficiency, high spectral efficiency, consistent gain, and 
large bandwidth. The challenge of the spiral antennas is the 
feeding mechanism. Spiral antennas are inherently 
wideband, but the feed structure must maintain a consistent 
impedance (often 50 Ω) across a wide frequency range. 
(b) Sinuous Antenna: 
It can be seen as a combination of spiral and log-periodic 
antenna concepts, which result in a radiating element 
capable of producing wideband radiation in the broadside 
direction with polarization diversity. Non-self-
complementary two-arm sinuous antenna and a self-
complementary conical projection of the two-arms sinuous 
structure were introduced to realize unique multiband 
behaviour with alternating polarization handedness between 
adjacent bands [10]. Sinuous antennas have complex 
geometries that make fabrication and miniaturization 
challenging. They exhibit moderate gain and bidirectional 
radiation, which may require additional reflectors. Feeding 
issues in sinuous antennas are critical challenges that can 
significantly affect their broadband performance, 
polarization purity, and radiation patterns. 
(c) Log-Periodic Dipole Antenna (LPDA): 
LPDAs offer wide bandwidth with stable radiation patterns 
and consistent gain across frequencies. They support both 
linear polarization and good impedance matching over large 
frequency ranges [11]. Log-periodic dipole antenna printed 
on a PCB has many advantages, including low 
manufacturing cost, a high yield rate, and lightweight.  The 
characteristic of this antenna changes with the logarithm of 
frequency, and expands the characteristic in one cycle to a 
wide channel. Normally, the log periodic antenna requires 
more elements for accepting and sending signals. 
Additionally, they are large and not suitable for systems 
with limited size or volume. It has a narrower beamwidth 
compared to other types of antennas. The assembly and 
maintenance of the log periodic antenna may be more 
complex and time-consuming due to the multiple elements 
and precise spacing required. 
(d) Vivaldi Antenna: 
The Vivaldi antenna is a type of end-ﬁre traveling-wave 
antenna that is highly suitable for applications requiring a 
broad bandwidth and directional characteristics [12]. The 
core design of Vivaldi antennas includes an exponentially 
tapered planar slot, typically fed through electromagnetic 
coupling by means of a microstrip-to-slot line transition. 
This antenna operates with a non-resonant radiation 
mechanism, where waves travel along the tapered slot, 
conﬁned between the conductors. 
In the design of a wideband antenna array tailored for 
seeker-based systems, several candidate antenna structures 
were evaluated, including spiral, sinuous, log-periodic 
dipole, and tapered slot (Vivaldi) antennas. Each 
configuration was assessed based on criteria such as 
bandwidth, gain, polarization stability, structural 
conformity, and suitability for compact integration. While 
spiral and sinuous antennas offer excellent bandwidth and 
polarization diversity, their bidirectional radiation and 
fabrication complexity pose limitations for conformal 
implementation. Similarly, log-periodic dipole arrays, 
though broadband, require larger apertures and are less 
planar. After a thorough comparative analysis, the Vivaldi 
antenna was selected as the optimal solution. Its inherent 
wideband characteristics, high directivity, low profile, and 
ability to conform to curved surfaces make it highly 
compatible with the compact, planar, and conformal 
constraints of seeker-based array applications.  
III. ANTENNA DESIGN 
On a 20 mil thick Rogers RT6035HTC substrate, with a loss 
tangent of 0.0023, a dielectric constant of 3.6, a Vivaldi-like 
antenna is designed in CST studio suite 2021. Considering 
the aforementioned design parameters into account, the 
basic structure of the proposed Vivaldi-like antenna using 
Co-planar waveguide (CPW) technology is demonstrated in 
Fig. 1. The considered antenna dimensions and their values 
are given in Table 1. The size of the antenna is 50×98 mm². 
Tapered slots are inserted to match the impedance at 
intermediate frequencies. Two small circular slots have been 
inserted between the feed arm and ground arm to cater to the 
radiation pattern at higher frequencies. The top layer 
indicates the exponential taper profile [7] which is defined 
by the opening rate, R and the two points P1 (x1,y1) and P2 
(X2,Y2). The exponential taper profile is given by: 
 
y = C1eR x + C₂   for   x₀ ≤ x ≤ x₁          (1) 
 
where R is the rate of opening and 
 
C₁ = (y₁ - y₀) / (eRx₁ - eRx₀)                      (2) 
 
 
C₂ = (eRx₁ y₀ - eRx₀ y₁) / (eRx₁ - eRx₀)         (3)

V. CONFORMAL ARRAY 
A 4×1 Vivaldi antenna structure has been conformally 
shaped to a cylinder of different diameters to see the 
impedance and radiation characteristics to be used for 
compact and high-performance wideband antenna systems. 
The configuration illustrated in Fig. 6 showcases an array of 
tapered slot antenna elements, integrated on a curved surface 
to suit the geometry of cylindrical platforms. The element 
layout and feeding network are carefully optimized to 
maintain consistent performance despite the surface 
curvature, enabling effective angle-of-arrival estimation and 
direction-finding capabilities. reflection coefficient (S 11) 
characteristics of the conformal Vivaldi-like antenna array 
were analyzed for three different cylindrical curvatures of 
diameters 23 cm, 27 cm, and 30 cm. The array maintains S 11 
below –10 dB across the operating spectrum as illustrated in 
Fig. 7. Furthermore, as the diameter increases, the resonant 
frequencies show a slight downward shift, which may be 
attributed to the impact of the larger curvature on the current 
distribution and effective electrical length.

Overall, these results suggest that the designed antenna can 
be a good candidate for conformal shapes.

The radiation patterns were also observed for the conformal 
case. Radiation patterns show a similar behaviour as seen in 
the planar array case. The current design will undergo 
further refinement to enhance its performance and 
adaptability for real-world applications. Subsequent stages 
will include fabrication and experimental testing to validate 
the simulated results. Additionally, efforts will be made to 
extend the antenna’s operating frequency range toward 2–
18 GHz, aiming to achieve broader bandwidth coverage and 
improved functional capabilities. These advancements are 
expected to yield more efficient results, particularly in 
applications requiring high-resolution wideband 
performance. 
VI. CONCLUSION 
A compact, planar, and conformal Vivaldi-like antenna and 
its array have been presented to meet the requirements of 
wideband, compact, and platform-adaptable systems, 
particularly suited for Electronic Warfare Applications 
seeker. The design demonstrates the ability to retain the 
desirable characteristics of traditional Vivaldi antennas, such 
as broadband operation and directional radiation, while 
conforming to a curved surface for better mechanical 
integration. The simulation results indicate promising 
performance, validating the potential of this configuration 
for practical use. Future enhancements, including bandwidth 
extension and side lobe reduction, are expected to further 
improve the antenna's effectiveness and readiness for real-
time deployment in advanced RF systems.
